# Synthesis Audit 01 — Persistence, Maintenance, and Quiescence

> **Question:** does the provisional thesis `persistence is often an activity disguised as a property` survive comparison with the five grounded retention regimes?

**Status:** bounded pre-synthesis audit.

This document does not add a new historical case and does not yet perform a Stiegler/Heidegger/Ernst synthesis. It tests one project thesis against mechanisms that are already grounded in primary evidence.

Grounded cases used here:

- [`00 — Abacus / reckoning retained position`](../cases/00-abacus-retained-position.md), with [`evidence/00-abacus-rod-line-reckoning-grounding.md`](../evidence/00-abacus-rod-line-reckoning-grounding.md);
- [`02 — Magnetic core destructive read`](../cases/02-magnetic-core-destructive-read.md), with [`evidence/02-magnetic-core-1951-1954-grounding.md`](../evidence/02-magnetic-core-1951-1954-grounding.md);
- [`03 — DRAM scheduled restoration`](../cases/03-dram-refresh-as-scheduled-restoration.md), with [`evidence/03-dram-1967-1982-grounding.md`](../evidence/03-dram-1967-1982-grounding.md);
- [`04 — Mapped Flash logical identity`](../cases/04-flash-virtual-mapping-logical-identity.md), with [`evidence/04-flash-1992-1998-grounding.md`](../evidence/04-flash-1992-1998-grounding.md);
- [`05 — RADOS replica agreement and repair`](../cases/05-rados-replicated-object-repair.md), with [`evidence/05-rados-2006-2007-grounding.md`](../evidence/05-rados-2006-2007-grounding.md).

The mercury delay-line case is deliberately excluded from the formal test because it remains `first-pass`. It may later strengthen the continuous-regeneration side of the comparison, but it is not needed to decide whether the current thesis overstates active maintenance.

---

## 1. Verdict

The thesis **survives only after qualification**.

The strong universal version is false:

> `Persistence is activity.`

The grounded passive-position, magnetic-core, and Flash cases are counterexamples at the substrate level. A bead can remain where it was placed; a ferrite core can retain a remanent state without external maintenance energy; a Flash cell can retain programmed state without periodic refresh merely to keep the charge/trapped state present.

The weaker project intuition is well supported:

> **Many forms of useful persistence are achieved through maintenance that a stable interface can hide.**

But the comparison shows that `maintenance` is not one thing and does not always operate continuously. The obligation can be:

- absent during quiescence;
- scheduled by physical decay;
- created by an access;
- created by rewrite/capacity pressure;
- created by wear or media failure;
- created by replica or membership failure;
- supplied by human interpretation and protection rather than autonomous machinery.

The revised thesis should therefore ask **which layer is being kept persistent, and what event creates the maintenance obligation**.

## 2. The five grounded regimes do not support one activity model

### 2.1 Passive position: persistence can be genuinely quiescent

Cheng Dawei's 1592 abacus instruction to leave the computed number unmoved and Adam Ries's 1525 line-reckoning comparison establish an operational state that remains available because a physical configuration stays in place.

The relevant retention mechanism is not repeated reconstruction. It is ordinary positional stability plus continued interpretability.

This is a direct counterexample to any claim that a retained state must be continually regenerated by a technical system.

However, the case also prevents the opposite overstatement `nothing is being maintained`:

- the configuration must be protected from disturbance;
- the positional convention must remain available;
- the operator must preserve procedural context;
- selection and interpretation are human-performed functions.

**Result:** substrate persistence can be quiescent while operational availability still depends on human/procedural maintenance.

### 2.2 Magnetic core: quiescent retention and access-triggered restoration coexist

The core grounding record establishes two facts that must be kept together.

First, Brown's 1953-filed nondestructive-sensing patent explicitly describes stable remanent states that can remain without external maintenance energy. Forrester's 1951-filed patent likewise grounds stable zero-excitation remanence.

Second, the bounded classic MIT scheme uses destructive read. Papian's 1953 machine paper makes read and rewrite part of one memory cycle; Forrester and Widrow likewise document the need to restore information after destructive sensing when it must remain available.

Thus the same storage regime can be:

```text
quiescent between accesses
        +
actively reconstructed after a particular access
```

**Result:** `active` versus `passive` is not a property of the technology as a whole. It can be a property of a phase in the object's operational life.

### 2.3 DRAM: the strongest case for constitutive scheduled maintenance

DRAM is the cleanest support for the original thesis.

Dennard's 1967-filed patent states that stored capacitor charge leaks and therefore must be periodically regenerated. AMD's 1976 Am9050 documentation combines a one-transistor/capacitor cell with mandatory refresh, while Intel AP-133 later makes the sense → amplify → return-to-cell reconstruction path explicit.

The important point is not simply that `refresh happens`.

Without service before the physical deadline, the represented distinction ceases to be recoverable. Scheduled reconstruction is therefore **constitutive of continued logical retention over intervals longer than the cell's unaided charge survival**.

**Result:** here persistence across the specified service interval really is an achieved process hidden behind a stable logical address.

### 2.4 Mapped Flash: nonvolatile state can still require maintenance for continued service

The mapped-Flash case is a second counterexample to a universal active-retention thesis at the cell level.

Ban's 1993-filed system assumes a nonvolatile medium. Nothing analogous to DRAM refresh is required merely to keep an unchanged programmed block present while idle.

But the service promised above that substrate has additional obligations:

- changed logical data are written out of place;
- mapping/allocation state must identify the current embodiment;
- obsolete embodiments are invalidated before later erase;
- still-current data are copied during reclamation;
- Wells's 1992-lineage patent adds wear distribution as a distinct lifetime objective;
- later bounded NAND evidence adds bad-block metadata, ECC, and replacement.

The trigger is therefore usually not `time passed while idle`. It is **workload, capacity pressure, wear, or failure**.

**Result:** nonvolatility can coexist with substantial maintenance, but that maintenance sustains logical identity, rewritability, capacity, and usable lifetime rather than the moment-to-moment existence of an unchanged cell state.

### 2.5 RADOS: repair is contingent, while currentness and commit are protocol achievements

The grounded 2006–2007 RADOS case adds another trigger structure.

Replica repair is not a fixed-period refresh cycle. It becomes necessary when failures, recoveries, expansion, contraction, or map changes alter which OSDs should hold a placement group. Peering, version/log comparison, and re-replication reconstruct a current replica set.

At the same time, ordinary write persistence has explicit thresholds: the 2006 design distinguishes replicated in-memory acknowledgement from later durable disk commit. The 2007 sources make map epochs, prior sets, PG logs, and stale-read exclusion part of deciding which physical copy may count as current.

**Result:** distributed persistence is partly a property of protocol transitions. `Maintenance` is event-driven repair, while `currentness` and `durability` are separately established protocol states.

## 3. Counterexample ledger

| Candidate claim | Result | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every persistent state requires continuous active maintenance. | **rejected** | passive position, quiescent core remanence, and idle Flash are counterexamples |
| A system can hide substantial work behind an apparently stable retained object. | **supported** | DRAM refresh, core restore, Flash remapping/reclamation, and RADOS replication/repair all do so in different ways |
| If a substrate is nonvolatile, persistence is maintenance-free. | **rejected** | core access can create restore work; Flash service continuity depends on mapping/reclamation and later failure management |
| Maintenance must be periodic to be constitutive of persistence. | **rejected** | core restore is access-triggered; Flash work is workload/capacity/wear-triggered; RADOS repair is failure/membership-triggered |
| No active substrate maintenance means no technical retention. | **rejected** | the grounded abacus/reckoning case supplies passive operational retained state |
| Hidden retention work is necessarily automated. | **rejected** | the passive-position case makes interpretation, protection, selection, and reset explicitly human-performed |
| `Active` and `passive` classify whole technologies cleanly. | **rejected** | core is quiescent at rest and reconstructive after destructive read; Flash is quiescent when idle but maintenance-heavy under rewrite/failure |
| A stable user-visible identity can conceal replacement of the physical embodiment. | **supported** | DRAM restoration, mapped Flash relocation, and RADOS replica replacement support different versions of this claim |

## 4. The thesis was conflating several retention targets

The phrase `persistence is maintained` becomes much more precise when the retained target is named.

| Retention target | Example | What must continue or remain valid? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **physical distinction** | core remanence; idle Flash state; bead position | substrate state itself |
| **logical value across physical reconstruction** | DRAM; destructive-read core | equivalence of the restored logical state |
| **logical identity across relocation** | mapped Flash | mapping/allocation relation that selects the current embodiment |
| **currentness / authority** | RADOS | version, map epoch, PG log, role and recovery state |
| **serviceability under future writes** | mapped Flash | free capacity, reclaimability, wear/failure management |
| **interpretability / procedural availability** | abacus / line reckoning | convention, context, operator competence and undisturbed configuration |
| **durable commit threshold** | RADOS | protocol evidence that acknowledged state has reached persistent media at the required replicas |

This suggests a general methodological rule:

> **Never say that `persistence requires maintenance` without specifying what is being kept persistent.**

A substrate may remain unchanged while addressability fails. Bytes may survive while currentness metadata is lost. A logical object may survive while every original physical embodiment is replaced. A physical configuration may survive while its procedural meaning disappears.

## 5. Maintenance-trigger taxonomy supported by the current cases

The grounded evidence supports at least the following trigger-sensitive vocabulary.

### Quiescent retention

The state can remain during an interval without a recurring restore operation merely to keep that physical distinction present.

Grounded examples: passive bead/counter position, ferrite-core remanence, unchanged Flash state.

This term does **not** mean `no labor, no control infrastructure, no failure modes`.

### Deadline-driven maintenance

A physical distinction has a bounded unaided lifetime, so the system must restore it before a deadline even if no useful access occurs.

Grounded example: DRAM refresh.

### Access-triggered restoration

The access operation itself disturbs or destroys the physical state and thereby creates an immediate restoration obligation when the logical value must remain available.

Grounded example: classic destructive-read magnetic core; bounded Dennard 1T1C embodiment also shows this independently of periodic regeneration.

### Workload / capacity-triggered maintenance

Updates consume writable physical opportunities or create obsolete embodiments; copying and erasure are required so the service can continue accepting rewrites.

Grounded example: mapped Flash reclamation.

### Wear / lifetime-triggered placement

Maintenance policy changes where work is performed because repeated switching/program/erase consumes finite usable life.

Grounded example: Wells's early-1990s Flash wear-leveling evidence.

### Failure / membership-triggered repair

Redundancy or placement ceases to satisfy the system's desired current configuration after a failure or topology change; the system reconstructs state onto replacement members.

Grounded example: RADOS peering and re-replication.

### Interpretive / procedural maintenance

Physical state remains, but useful recovery depends on conventions, procedural role, skilled selection, or contextual knowledge.

Grounded example: abacus / line reckoning.

These categories may overlap in one system. They are **maintenance obligations**, not mutually exclusive technology classes.

---

## 6. Revised thesis

The original wording remains a useful provocation, but the evidence now supports a more discriminating version:

> **Persistence is often an achieved relation, not a maintenance-free property. Some retained states remain quiescently; others require scheduled reconstruction, access-triggered restore, remapping, or repair. The first question is therefore not whether persistence is `active` or `passive`, but which layer is being kept persistent and what event creates its maintenance obligation.**

This revision preserves the strongest insight of the original thesis — stable interfaces can hide work — while incorporating the passive-position and remanent-state counterexamples instead of treating them as exceptions to be explained away.

---

## 7. What this audit does **not** establish

This file deliberately does not claim that:

- every form of `technical retention` belongs to one metaphysical category;
- maintenance is the essence of memory;
- human procedural work and automated controller work are historically continuous forms of the same institution;
- DRAM refresh, Flash reclamation, and distributed repair are technically equivalent;
- Stieglerian tertiary retention is identical to any of these operational states;
- Heideggerian `Bestand` is clarified merely by observing hidden maintenance;
- Ernst's storage/transfer proposition has already been proven or rejected.

Those are later philosophical questions. This audit only repairs the engineering premise they would have to confront.

## 8. Consequence for the next synthesis pass

Thesis 1 can now be treated as **audited and revised**, not promoted to a final conclusion.

The next bounded synthesis question should be README thesis 2:

> `Storage is temporal transport.`

That claim needs a stricter test because it may be true in a way so general that it stops distinguishing delay, quiescent retention, periodic reconstruction, relocation, and distributed repair. The next pass should ask what explanatory work `temporal transport` performs that `state remains recoverable at t1` does not already perform.
