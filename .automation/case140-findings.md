## Case 140 — IBM double-disk-failure array-code findings

Grounding record: [`evidence/140-ibm-1991-1994-double-disk-array-codes-grounding.md`](evidence/140-ibm-1991-1994-double-disk-array-codes-grounding.md).

- **2965 — 1991 patent priority != 1991 public disclosure:** EP0499365A2 records a 1991-02-11 priority for an IBM up-to-two-unavailable-DASD coding/rebuild family, while the inspected EP publication is dated 1992-08-19. (`H/P*`, `X`)
- **2966 — ICC 1992 explicitly targets two disk failures with two redundant disks:** IBM Research's record for Blaum's paper states protection against two failures, two redundant disks, and XOR-only encoding/decoding. (`H/P`)
- **2967 — two redundant disks != two payload replicas:** the inspected schemes retain coding relations distributed across data/redundant members rather than two complete copies of each logical object. (`H/P`, `E`, `X`)
- **2968 — one independent parity relation != enough for two arbitrary missing members:** Case 17's single-parity reconstruction solves one missing member; two arbitrary unavailable members require additional independent redundancy structure. (`E`, `A`)
- **2969 — two-failure coding can retain residual failure margin after the first member loss:** within the stated up-to-two-unavailable-member model, one failed member can leave both current reconstructability and capacity to survive one further located member loss. (`H/P`, `E`)
- **2970 — recoverable now != full designed failure margin != repair complete:** a first failure reduces a two-failure code's remaining margin even while data remain reconstructable; rebuilding onto a spare restores the designed physical redundancy embodiment. (`H/P*`, `E`)
- **2971 — known failed/unavailable-member reconstruction != arbitrary silent-corruption detection:** the historical patent distinguishes failures/unavailability, errors, and erasures; coded recovery of located missing members does not prove automatic location/correction of every silent error. (`H/P*`, `E`, `X`)
- **2972 — double-failure protection != scrub/integrity verification:** reconstructability does not itself establish proactive checking, checksum freshness, or discovery coverage for latent corruption. (`E`, `X`)
- **2973 — EVENODD 1994 is explicitly framed for RAID architectures but not thereby source-era `RAID-6`:** the inspected title/abstract says `RAID architectures` and `double disk failures`; later RAID-6 nomenclature must not be projected backward without evidence. (`H/P`, `X`)
- **2974 — later RAID-6 any-two-device terminology is functional comparison, not genealogy:** FAST 2008 supplies an explicit k+2 / any-two-device RAID-6 category, useful for comparison without proving that the 1991–1994 IBM sources used that label or directly caused later standards. (`A`, `X`)
- **2975 — authors' `first known` optimality claim != independently established universal priority:** EVENODD's 1994 abstract scopes its claim to double-failure storage-and-performance optimality and itself acknowledges prior two-extra-disk Reed–Solomon approaches. (`H/P`, `X`)
- **2976 — XOR-only implementation != RAID-5-equivalent protection:** reuse of XOR/parity computational primitives does not collapse the extra independent redundancy relation that changes failure tolerance. (`H/P`, `E`, `X`)
- **2977 — parity-hardware compatibility != named deployed controller support:** the EVENODD abstract's claim that standard RAID-5 parity hardware could implement the scheme is not a shipment, firmware, or conformance record. (`H/P`, `X`)
- **2978 — storage/computation optimality != rebuild wall-clock/service optimality:** the historical optimality wording does not establish minimum rebuild duration, IOPS interference, exposure to correlated failures, or operational risk. (`H/P`, `X`)
- **2979 — Case 17 single-parity degraded mode != Case 140 residual second-failure margin:** both separate degraded service from completed repair, but the bounded two-failure code retains one further located-member-loss margin after the first failure. (`A`, `E`)
- **2980 — reconstructability relation != rebuild scheduling policy:** Case 140 grounds whether missing content is mathematically reconstructable; Case 136 separately grounds controller resource priority and repair admission/execution behavior. (`A`, `X`)
- **2981 — spare rebuild != sanitization:** restoring coded state onto replacement capacity does not prove overwrite, erase, crypto erase, or forensic disappearance of the failed/removed member. (`E`, `X`)
- **2982 — related-repository boundary:** fresh `tmzncty/computing-archaeology` searches found no dedicated EVENODD / RAID double-parity study; broad Reed–Solomon→array-code genealogy, RAID-6 naming, controller adoption, and production history belong there if developed, while Case 140 retains the narrower reconstructability/residual-margin comparison. (`H/P` project-state record)
