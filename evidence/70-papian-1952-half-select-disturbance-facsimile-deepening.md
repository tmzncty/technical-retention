## Historical record

### 1. Repeated nonselecting pulses are an explicit state-disturbance test

In the article's `INFORMATION-RETENTION AND SIGNAL RATIOS` section, Papian defines undisturbed ONE and ZERO states at the two remanent-flux directions and then describes the effect of the `H_M/2` pulse applied to cores elsewhere in the array during selection.

Repeated nonselecting read pulses applied to a core containing a ONE move the operating point along the B axis and can disturb or destroy the stored information. For a satisfactory core with suitable hysteresis shape and correctly adjusted parameters, however, the operating point moves toward an asymptotic position not far above the original `-B_R` remanent point rather than continuing without bound.

Papian then calls the result after a large number of such pulses a **disturbed ONE**. Its output is usually lower than the undisturbed-ONE output.

The complementary pattern also matters: repeated nonselecting write-ONE pulses move the operating point of a ZERO from `+B_R` downward, so the disturbed-ZERO output is usually larger than the undisturbed-ZERO output.

**Historical-record claim:** the 1952 paper does not treat disturbance only as a binary “flipped / not flipped” event. It measures how repeated nonselecting traffic changes the later readout of both stored states.

### 2. The discriminability metric is the disturbed-signal ratio

Papian defines the **disturbed-signal ratio** as the disturbed-ONE output divided by the disturbed-ZERO output. Because disturbance can lower the ONE output while increasing the ZERO output, this ratio is a direct criterion for whether the two logical values remain separable at readout.

The paper says the ideal disturbed-signal ratio approaches infinity and that practical operation requires it to be much greater than one.

A separate **nonselecting signal ratio** compares disturbed-ONE output with output generated by a nonselecting pulse itself; this too is treated as a critical readout criterion.

**Historical-record claim:** retention quality in Papian's bounded experiment is partly operationalized by preserved output separation, not only by the continued existence of remanent flux with the intended sign.

### 3. Disturbance exposure is experimentally multidimensional

Papian's test technique interposes a large number of half-amplitude, nonselecting pulses between a write-ONE pulse and a read pulse for the disturbed-ONE mode; other modes test disturbed and undisturbed ZERO states.

The article explicitly states that the following are independently variable:

- pulse amplitude;
- pulse length;
- spacing;
- number of intervening nonselecting pulses.

This is important evidence against reducing `half-select exposure` to one universal counter.

**Historical-record claim:** Papian's own test apparatus treats disturbance as a function of several pulse parameters, not simply elapsed time or number of neighboring accesses.

### 4. Too much selecting/current amplitude can collapse the readout margin

Papian reports scope tests of a ferritic core at an optimum magnetizing-force amplitude and at an amplitude that is too large. With excessive amplitude, disturbed-ZERO output increases while disturbed-ONE output decreases, yielding an unsatisfactory disturbed-signal ratio close to one.

That observation supplies a direct period example of a core remaining inside a coincident-current experiment while the binary readout margin becomes inadequate.

The paper's summary table reports for the best metallic core a disturbed-signal ratio of `13`, a nonselecting-signal ratio of `16`, and a ONE response time of about `25 microseconds`. Those are properties of the tested core and pulse conditions, not universal magnetic-core-memory constants.

## Engineering reconstruction

### A. Half-select exposure is not one scalar quantity

The facsimile supports a more precise engineering model:

```text
half-select disturbance exposure
    = f(
        pulse amplitude,
        pulse width,
        pulse spacing,
        pulse count,
        starting logical state,
        hysteresis-loop shape,
        surrounding read/write sequence
      )
```

This is a reconstruction from the paper's test variables and state-dependent observations. Papian does not present that equation.

Therefore:

```text
same number of nonselecting pulses
!=
same disturbance history
```

unless the other relevant pulse/core conditions are also controlled.

### B. An asymptotic disturbed state is not automatically a safe state

Papian's satisfactory-core discussion says repeated nonselecting pulses can approach an asymptotic operating point. That bounds one kind of excursion, but it does not by itself establish adequate readout margin.

The same article separately requires a disturbed-signal ratio much greater than one and shows that an over-large drive amplitude can push the ratio close to one.

So:

```text
disturbance approaches an asymptote
!=
binary discrimination remains adequate
```

The limiting state still has to satisfy the readout criterion.

### C. Retained polarity, retained margin, and recoverable symbol are different relations

For this bounded case, three questions must remain separate:

1. Does the remanent-flux polarity remain the intended one?
2. How far has repeated nonselecting excitation displaced the operating point / reduced margin?
3. Are disturbed ONE and ZERO outputs still sufficiently distinguishable through the sensing path?

The facsimile makes the second and third questions experimentally visible even before one asserts a permanent stable-state reversal.

This strengthens the Case-70 distinction:

```text
state not fully reversed
!=
state unaffected
!=
symbol reliably recoverable
```

## Functional analogy boundary

Cases 52, 53, and 59 remain useful only at a high functional level:

- NAND read disturb: a read aimed at one region can stress retained neighboring/non-target state;
- DRAM RowHammer: repeated activation can endanger physically adjacent retained charge;
- NAND program interference: programming can alter neighbors' threshold distributions.

Papian's mechanism is not those mechanisms. It concerns ferrite hysteresis, coincident/half-amplitude magnetizing pulses, and signal discrimination. No RowHammer/NAND genealogy, material identity, or shared quantitative threshold is inferred.

The direct facsimile actually makes the stop condition stronger: Papian independently varies pulse amplitude, width, spacing, and count, so even the abstract phrase `repeated access-induced disturbance` is too coarse to transport quantitative rules between technologies.

## Philosophical / project interpretation — explicitly later

A narrow project-level interpretation is now better grounded:

> **technical forgetting can begin as loss of distinguishability before it becomes complete loss or inversion of the retained physical state.**

This is not Papian's historical vocabulary. The historical vocabulary is `disturbed ONE`, `disturbed ZERO`, `disturbed-signal ratio`, `nonselecting signal ratio`, and repeated `nonselecting` pulses.

The interpretation is useful only because the engineering record distinguishes physical state, disturbance margin, and recoverable symbol.

## Rejected claims and stop conditions

This deepening does **not** establish that:

- all half-selected cores eventually flip under ordinary operation;
- one universal pulse count predicts magnetic-core disturbance;
- the tested best metallic or ferritic core represents every production memory;
- an asymptotic operating point guarantees safe readout;
- Papian's laboratory pulse patterns are a complete workload model for a named production machine;
- magnetic-core half-select disturbance is the technical ancestor of DRAM RowHammer or NAND disturb;
- a disturbed-signal ratio close to one is a media-sanitization event;
- Papian invented coincident-current magnetic memory or was the first person to identify partial-select disturbance.

Named-machine quantitative margins, production distributions, temperature dependence, and invention-priority genealogy remain separate evidence debts.
