# Grounding record: passive positional retention in Chinese and European calculation

## Scope

This record deepens [`cases/00-abacus-retained-position.md`](../cases/00-abacus-retained-position.md) without turning it into a general history of the abacus or of premodern arithmetic.

It tests three bounded claims:

1. late-Ming suanpan procedure directly documents a numerical configuration that is deliberately kept in place for later operational use;
2. older Chinese counting-rod vocabulary supports spatially organized procedural calculation, but the exact material operation must not be inferred more strongly than the evidence permits;
3. a non-Chinese line-reckoning source independently shows an operationally transformed positional counter field, making passive positional working retention a defensible **functional comparison** rather than a claim unique to the Chinese bead frame.

The target is the repository's passive-position synthesis gate, not a genealogy from ancient counters to modern computer registers.

## 1. Cheng Dawei, 1592: direct facsimile anchor for retained working state

### Source inspected

Cheng Dawei (程大位), *新編直指筭法統宗* / *算法統宗*, volume 1, 1592.

A public digital edition at Source Library identifies the work as the 1592 volume and supplies an image of each original scan. The directly inspected image for digital scan **p. 70 / 82** contains the section heading:

> `直指定位訣`

The relevant lines are visibly present in the scan and transcribed by the site as:

> `預先以算盤上寫定萬千百十...因乘完畢待數莫動`

The bounded historical content is clear even without relying on the site's English translation:

- `算盤` names the abacus directly;
- positions are designated in advance for decimal or metrological units;
- after the multiplication procedure is complete, the resulting number is instructed to remain unmoved (`待數莫動`).

This is stronger than the first-pass case's OCR-only anchor because the page image itself was inspected.

### Exact digital anchor

- Source Library, *Systematic Treatise on Arithmetic, Vol. 1*, scan p. 70 / 82, `直指定位訣`: <https://sourcelibrary.org/book/suanfa-tongzong-systematic-treatise-on-arithmetic-vol-1-dawei/page-number/70>
- Source Library book record: <https://sourcelibrary.org/book/suanfa-tongzong-systematic-treatise-on-arithmetic-vol-1-dawei>

A separately catalogued National Library of China 1592 volume is available through Wikimedia Commons, but this grounding pass does **not** silently equate Source Library's digital page number with an NLC folio/page number. The Source Library scan is the exact anchor checked here.

### Claim consequences

**H/P — strong:** Cheng's text directly documents deliberate position assignment on a `算盤` and deliberate preservation of the completed numerical configuration rather than immediate clearing.

**E — strong:** a completed configured surface can therefore function as retained working state across the interval between one completed operation and subsequent inspection/reinterpretation/use.

**A — bounded:** this is `register-like` only in the functional sense that an operational value remains available. Nothing in this evidence establishes the historical concept `register`, a CPU-like architecture, or a direct technological genealogy.

### Why this matters for the retention thesis

The central fact is not merely that beads are physically stable. A chair is physically stable too. The retained configuration is explicitly embedded in a procedure that:

1. assigns representational meaning to positions;
2. transforms those positions in arithmetic;
3. tells the operator not to disturb the resulting number at a defined procedural moment.

That combination makes **passive positional retention** narrower and more defensible than the claim that every stable external arrangement is `memory`.

## 2. Older Chinese counting-rod material: recover vocabulary, preserve uncertainty

### Primary procedural vocabulary

The *Nine Chapters on Mathematical Procedures* provides direct textual evidence for spatial procedure even where the physical instrument is not described in enough detail to reconstruct every motion safely.

In the `方程` procedure, the text instructs the operator:

> `置...於右方。中、左禾列如右方。`

The important period verbs are `置` (place/set down) and `列` (arrange in a row/array), together with explicitly named right, middle, and left positions.

A convenient searchable transcription is:

- *九章算術*, `方程`: <https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SBCK080/chapter/SBCK080_15>

This textual evidence establishes **spatially organized procedure**. It does not, by itself, prove every detail of the counting surface or rod-handling reconstruction.

### Specialist reconstruction and vocabulary

Yiwen Zhu's open-access study, “The interplay between textual procedures and material operations from the viewpoint of Chinese mathematical texts,” *Science in Context* 36(3), published online 9 October 2025, is especially useful because it makes the evidence problem explicit rather than hiding it.

Zhu notes that historians commonly understand these procedures as operations with counting rods (`筭籌`, *suan chou*), while also emphasizing that **direct evidence for how the rods were used is limited**. The evidence base is largely procedural texts and later counting diagrams.

The article also recovers several useful historical terms from bounded contexts:

- `筭籌` — counting rods;
- `借筭` — literally a “borrowed counting rod” in a root-extraction procedure;
- `等` — a positional/rank term in *The Nine Chapters*, whose exact interpretation has attracted scholarly disagreement;
- `位` — place/position in *Master Sun*;
- `筭圖` — counting diagrams in later mathematical writings.

Source:

- Yiwen Zhu, “The interplay between textual procedures and material operations from the viewpoint of Chinese mathematical texts,” *Science in Context* 36(3), DOI 10.1017/S0269889725100860: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/science-in-context/article/interplay-between-textual-procedures-and-material-operations-from-the-viewpoint-of-chinese-mathematical-texts/7E654BC8863452F26F2E0C43892699F4>

### Historiographic boundary

This evidence **does not authorize** a smooth story such as:

```text
ancient counting rods
    -> same operational semantics
Ming abacus
    -> register
modern CPU
```

Zhu's analysis is valuable precisely because the material operation cannot always be read mechanically off the surviving text. Even the physical surface is historically variable: the article notes evidence for rods being operated on the ground in Han contexts and on desks in Song contexts, while extant sources do not simply hand us a marked address grid for those surfaces.

Accordingly:

- **H/P:** early texts establish procedural placement, ordering, movement, and positional vocabulary;
- **H/S:** specialist history connects those texts to counting-rod operations and reconstructs likely material practices;
- **X:** an uninterrupted, directly documented rod-board-abacus genealogy with identical state semantics has not been established here.

This strengthens rather than weakens the case. The repository can compare retained-position mechanisms while keeping the historical actor's vocabulary and the modern reconstruction separate.

## 3. Adam Ries, 1525: independent European line reckoning

### Source inspected

Adam Ries (Riese), *Rechnung auff der Linihen*, Erfurt, 1525.

A Columbia University / Plimpton Library copy is digitized through the Internet Archive and available as a 100-page public-domain PDF through Wikimedia Commons.

Metadata and scan:

- Wikimedia Commons file record: <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rechnung_auff_der_Linihen_(IA_ldpd_13272232_000).pdf>
- Original PDF: <https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/03/Rechnung_auff_der_Linihen_%28IA_ldpd_13272232_000%29.pdf>

The directly inspected scan sequence around PDF image indices **12–13** shows:

- a horizontal line-and-space value schema;
- counters/numerals arranged at different vertical positions with different values;
- the transition into the section headed `Addirn / odder summiren`;
- subsequent textual instructions for arithmetic on the laid-out counter field.

Because the historical leaves are unnumbered, this record uses the digital PDF image index rather than inventing a printed page number.

### Institutional contextual check

The Adam-Ries-Bund describes Ries's first reckoning book as devoted to `Rechnen auf den Linien`: counters (`Rechenpfennige` or stones) are placed on a line schema / reckoning board and transformed during calculation. This institutional description is useful context, but the primary scan remains the stronger source for what the 1525 book depicts.

- Adam-Ries-Bund, “1. Rechenbuch — Rechnung auff der Linihen”: <https://www.adam-ries-bund.de/forschung/erstes/>

### What the comparison establishes

**H/P:** a 1525 European arithmetic manual directly depicts and instructs positional calculation on a line schema using an operationally transformed counter field.

**E:** the field's current arrangement remains materially available between successive manual transformations. This is another case of passive positional working state.

**A:** Cheng's suanpan and Ries's line reckoning can be compared as two **different physical realizations of positional working retention**.

The comparison is useful because the media are not identical:

| Dimension | Cheng / suanpan | Ries / line reckoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| counter constraint | beads constrained by rods/frame | counters placed on an open line schema |
| positional convention | abacus columns/units explicitly designated | lines and intervening spaces carry value |
| transformation | bead movement | counter placement/removal/conversion |
| retention work | preserve current bead configuration | preserve current counter layout |
| machine-autonomous reading | no | no |

The bead frame constrains legal positions more mechanically than an open reckoning board. That difference is analytically useful: **retention can depend not only on a stable substrate but also on how strongly the medium constrains valid states.**

### What the comparison does not establish

`X` — no transmission claim is made between the Chinese and European systems.

`X` — no claim is made that either tradition possessed the modern concepts of a register, storage hierarchy, or machine memory.

`X` — chronological coexistence in the sixteenth century is not evidence of shared origin or technical influence.

The non-Chinese comparison is therefore a mechanism comparison, not a genealogy.

## 4. Grounded retention claims

After this pass, the following claims no longer depend on one OCR transcription or a modern museum summary.

### G00-1 — passive positional working retention exists as a historical procedure

**Evidence:** Cheng 1592 scan p. 70 directly couples designated abacus positions with an instruction to leave the completed number unmoved.

**Classification:** `H/P + E`.

### G00-2 — the meaningful retained state is relational

A bead/counter by itself is not the retained numerical state. The operative state is a relation among counter position, surface/frame geometry, positional convention, and procedure.

**Evidence:** Cheng's explicit position designation; Ries's line/space value schema.

**Classification:** `E`, strongly constrained by `H/P` evidence.

### G00-3 — passive retention need not be machine-autonomous

Both bounded examples require a human operator for selection, interpretation, transformation, protection from disturbance, and reset.

**Classification:** `E`.

This is a boundary result for the repository: **machine-autonomous readout is not a necessary condition for every form of technical retention**, though it becomes a major differentiator in later memory systems.

### G00-4 — positional retention is not uniquely Chinese or uniquely bead-based

Ries's 1525 line reckoning supplies an independent non-Chinese case in which an arithmetic procedure is carried by a materially persistent counter layout.

**Classification:** `H/P + A`.

### G00-5 — older rod practice must remain epistemically layered

Early Chinese procedural language supplies real evidence for placement and positional manipulation, but the exact material operation is partly reconstructed from specialist scholarship and later diagrams.

**Classification:** `H/P + H/S`; overstrong direct-material claims are rejected.

## 5. Counterexamples and limits after grounding

Grounding does not erase the boundary problems that make this case useful.

1. **Stable position alone is insufficient.** A stable object becomes relevant to this project only when its configuration participates in a representational/operational convention.
2. **Human-readable retained state is not automatically machine memory.** The operator supplies functions that later systems automate.
3. **Open-board and bead-frame configurations are not identical.** The degree of mechanical constraint differs.
4. **Counting-rod material operations are partly reconstructed.** Surviving procedural texts do not justify invented certainty about every physical movement or surface marking.
5. **Functional resemblance is not genealogy.** Neither the Chinese nor European example establishes direct descent into modern registers or memory architecture.
6. **Current-state retention is not history retention.** Once counters/beads are changed, the previous configuration is normally gone unless separately recorded.
